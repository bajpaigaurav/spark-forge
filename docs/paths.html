Reading paths

Four ways through the book

Nobody arrives wanting all thirty-one chapters in order. They arrive with an afternoon and a problem.

Each path is a different argument, not the same table of contents shuffled. What is left out matters as much as what is kept, so every path says what it skips and why that is safe. Times are computed from the real word count of the chapters included, at about 220 words a minute.

Skips 01-09, and that is the whole point of this path. The quadratic wall is restated inside 10 with the numbers that actually bite, so 09 is a story you have already heard. What is left is the cache as a subject in its own right: why it grows, how it is shared, how it is paged, how it is reused across requests, and how attention is computed without ever materialising it. 14 and 15 are included rather than skipped because they are cache-adjacent arguments a serving reader will be asked about. Closes on 29 and 30 so the claims are arithmetic and measurement, not folklore.

Skips 01-09 and every chapter from 11 to 25 except 12 and 13. You are not choosing a token; you are deciding how many requests fit. 10 is the memory budget, 12 and 13 are the two things you can actually deploy that change it, 26-28 are the scheduler and the sharding, and 29-31 tell you whether any of it can hit your latency target. 11 is cut because architecture choice is made before you arrive; 14 and 15 are cut because kernel selection does not change the cost model you are building here.
