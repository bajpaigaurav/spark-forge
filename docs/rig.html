Reproduce this

The book's trust rests on numbers scraped from one machine on one day. Here is what was measured, what came from a published source, and what you can check yourself.

Every number in the last part of the book traces to content/sources.json. Each entry there records a value, the string it was read from, and what it means. This page lists them, split by where they came from, and ships the artifacts that let a reader re-run the measurements.

34 of the 36 entries were read off this rig: counters scraped from the engine's /metrics on 2026-10-31, two hand-run probes, and the arithmetic that follows from them. The remaining 2 are published constants, not measurements.

The artifacts

Plain text, no build step. Download them and read them.

  • README.mdWhat this artifact is, what it is not, and why the raw metrics dump is deliberately absent.
  • serve-argv.txtThe serve command line and container environment. The four constants are transcribed from sources.json, each with the source string it came from quoted above it.
  • probe.shThe commands: the nvidia-smi clock query, the /metrics scrape, the sequential-vs-concurrent probe, and the arithmetic. Read it before running it.

What you can and cannot reproduce

You cannot reproduce the hardware.
The 273 GB/s of LPDDR5X bandwidth and the 3,003 MHz silicon maximum are properties of an NVIDIA DGX Spark (GB10). The 2,197 MHz observed clock is that part on that day, under that load. A different box gives different numbers, and that is the point of the book's own caveat.
You cannot reproduce the date.
The counters are one 98-request window on 2026-10-31. The workload was whatever that machine was serving then. Your traffic will not match it, so your averages will not match the book's.
You cannot reproduce the raw scrape, because it is not shipped.
The dump itself is not in this repository, and it was not reconstructed. A dump rebuilt from memory would look checkable while proving nothing. If you want the counters, scrape your own with probe.sh and run the same arithmetic.
You can reproduce the commands.
Every step in probe.sh is a plain HTTP GET, an nvidia-smi query, or a curl POST. The metric names are this engine's, so expect to edit the URL and the names before anything returns a number.
You can reproduce the arithmetic.
The pool arithmetic is exact: 262,144 / 4 = 65,536; 65,536 − 32,768 − 16 − 2 = 32,750; and 1,048,576 / 4 = 262,144, then − 32,768 − 16 − 2 = 229,358. Those are pure integer arithmetic on constants recorded in sources.json and come out the same on any machine. The throughput ratios are arithmetic too, but the two decode figures (59.1 and 106.2) came from the engine's own token counts rather than from 1,600 divided by the wall clock, so a reader's own division will land a few tenths away.
